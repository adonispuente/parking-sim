# Lot A parking features: research checklist

**Purpose:** visual inventory checklist for an approximate Lot A layout. This is not a surveyed plan, code-compliance review, or construction design. **Research checked:** 4 Nov 2026. ADA requirements below are federal baseline guidance; applicable state/local rules and site-specific design must be checked separately.

## Features to look for (typical inventory)

- **Stalls and markings:** locate each painted stall bank, its orientation, row ends, and any special-use markings. Record the drive aisle serving each bank and the apparent direction of circulation. FHWA's MUTCD governs traffic-control devices, including pavement markings and signs on public streets and site roadways open to public travel; verify which state/local MUTCD applies to the site. [FHWA MUTCD, current edition](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/index.htm)
- **Drive aisles / internal circulation:** trace drive lanes between stall banks, connections between lot sections, and vehicle entrances/exits; note apparent turning/circulation paths, but do not infer dimensions or one-way operation from a still image. [FHWA MUTCD](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/index.htm)
- **Accessible parking and access aisles:** identify designated accessible stalls, signs/symbols, and adjacent marked access aisles; note whether the aisle connects to the pedestrian route. Federal ADA standards specify minimum counts per parking facility and technical requirements for spaces, access aisles, and routes; counts and compliance cannot be established from an aerial photo alone. [U.S. Access Board, Parking Spaces guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-5-parking/) · [2010 ADA Standards, §502](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/)
- **Accessible pedestrian route and crossings:** trace a continuous pedestrian connection from accessible parking/site arrival points to accessible building entrances; where it crosses vehicle lanes, look for marked crossings and curb ramps. A crossing may be desirable/important but cannot be assumed present or compliant from faint aerial markings. [U.S. Access Board, Accessible Routes guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-4-accessible-routes/) · [2010 ADA Standards, §502 advisory](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/)
- **Landscape / refuge islands:** map raised or planted islands, medians, tree beds, end caps, and landscaped edge buffers that interrupt or guide vehicle rows. Treat exact island edges and curb details as approximate where trees/shadows obscure them. (Inventory guidance; no Lot A-specific design requirement inferred.)
- **Entrances and edge connections:** mark each visible driveway connection between Lot A, the internal circulation road, and surrounding access roads; distinguish vehicle access from pedestrian access. Traffic signs/markings, where applicable, should be checked against the current MUTCD and local adoption. [FHWA MUTCD](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/index.htm)
- **Signs:** look for accessible-space identification, directional/wayfinding, stop/yield, speed, and lot-use signs. Record only signs actually legible/visible; aerial imagery usually cannot establish sign content or placement compliance. [U.S. Access Board, Parking Spaces guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-5-parking/) · [FHWA MUTCD](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/index.htm)
- **Lighting:** look for pole-mounted luminaires and their approximate positions; confirm at ground level or from site plans because poles may be hidden by trees or indistinguishable in imagery. **Not confirmed for Lot A.**
- **Drainage:** look for inlets/grates, curb openings, swales, permeable/bioretention areas, and grading cues; aerial imagery alone does not establish drainage flow or underground systems. **Not confirmed for Lot A.**

## Evidence / use limits

- The Access Board’s official guide explains accessible parking scoping, location, identification, access aisles, and routes; the ADA Standards specify aisle and surface requirements. A schematic should show candidate locations/routes only, not claim compliance absent measurements and jurisdiction review. [Access Board guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-5-parking/) · [ADA Standards §502](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/)
- The Access Board guide says an accessible route connects accessible parking/site arrival points to accessible facility entrances; its parking advisory notes that marked crossings enhance pedestrian safety when the route crosses vehicular lanes. [Accessible Routes guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-4-accessible-routes/) · [ADA Standards §502](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/)
- FHWA describes the MUTCD as national standards for traffic-control devices, including markings and signs, on streets and site roadways open to public travel; revision/adoption may vary by jurisdiction, so confirm local applicability. [FHWA MUTCD](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/index.htm)
